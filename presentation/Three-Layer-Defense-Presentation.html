The Challenge: AI Adoption Outpaces Security

Current Reality

  • AI usage in workplace expanding faster than controls
  • Employees using AI to write code, access data, investigate systems
  • "Shadow AI" tools operating outside company control
  • Anyone can now code, regardless of technical background

The Governance Dilemma

  • Banning AI = Lost productivity benefits
  • Ungoverned AI = Data leakage, toxic content, exploitation
  • EDR systems flag AI behavior as suspicious
  • Non-technical users may misuse AI capabilities
"No single control is sufficient - good governance requires defense in depth."
2

The Solution: Three-Layer Defense-in-Depth

1

Organizational Policy

Defines what is and isn't allowed based on NIST AI RMF and OECD guidelines

2

Identity-Based Authorization

Controls WHO can access AI services using Microsoft Entra ID

3

Prompt-Level Defense

Filters WHAT users can do with Amazon Bedrock Guardrails & Flows

Policy (Rules)
→
Identity (Who)
→
Prompt (What)
→
Safe AI Usage

Layer 1: Organizational AI Policy

NIST AI Risk Management Framework

  • GOVERN - Establish AI governance
  • MAP - Understand system capabilities
  • MEASURE - Assess performance
  • MANAGE - Address identified risks

OECD AI Principles

  • Inclusive growth & well-being
  • Human rights & democratic values
  • Transparency & explainability
  • Robustness, safety & security
  • Accountability
4

Layer 1: Cultural & Implementation Considerations

Cultural/Regional Factors

  • 195 countries with distinct laws and cultures
  • US: Individualist approach - personal responsibility
  • Japan: Collectivist - consensus-based decisions
  • GDPR and data protection laws vary by region
  • Policy permitted at HQ may be prohibited abroad

Implementation Strategy

  • Select single approved AI provider
  • Block unauthorized AI via proxy/firewall policies
  • Use Amazon Bedrock for data isolation
  • Corporate data never trains external models
  • Strong encryption & fine-grained access controls
5

Layer 2: Identity-Based Authorization

Microsoft Entra ID - Zero Trust Access Control

1. Adaptive Risk Assessment

  • ML-based authentication signal analysis
  • Detect anomalous sign-ins
  • Assign risk levels automatically
  • Works for humans AND AI agents

2. Strong Authentication

  • Phishing-resistant credentials
  • Windows Hello for Business
  • FIDO2 security keys
  • AI agents as principals with access criteria

3. Automated Remediation

  • Auto-trigger MFA challenges
  • Self-service password reset (SSPR)
  • Risk reduction without human intervention
  • Block sign-in for high-risk scenarios

4. ITDR (Threat Detection)

  • Security Copilot integration
  • Natural-language threat queries
  • Categorized risk reports
  • Distinguish real threats vs. false positives

Layer 3: Prompt-Level Defenses

Amazon Bedrock Guardrails

Content Filters

  • Hate speech
  • Insults
  • Sexual content
  • Violence
  • Misconduct
  • Prompt attacks

Topic & Word Filters

  • Denied topics (e.g., illegal advice)
  • Custom word blocks
  • Profanity filtering
  • Competitor name blocking

Data Protection

  • PII detection & masking
  • SSN, DOB, addresses
  • Regex pattern matching
  • Contextual grounding checks
  • Hallucination detection
Enforcement operates in BOTH directions - checking inputs AND outputs

Limitations & Considerations

Shadow AI Persistence

  • Personal devices bypass corporate controls
  • Mobile networks, home connections
  • VPN/tethering workarounds
  • Raises effort to evade, doesn't eliminate

Prompt Control Scope

  • Only protects AI services through Bedrock
  • Cannot check external consumer AI products
  • Effectiveness depends on configuration
  • Content filtering is probabilistic

Trade-offs

  • Overly strict = blocks legitimate activity
  • Security vs. usability balance
  • Each organization must calibrate

Platform Specificity

  • Examples use Microsoft & AWS
  • Concepts adaptable to other platforms
  • AI security features evolving rapidly
12

Conclusions

  • No single control can bridge the AI governance gap
  • Three-layer defense-in-depth provides comprehensive coverage
  • Layer 1 (Policy): Establishes rules based on NIST/OECD frameworks
  • Layer 2 (Identity): Controls access at user/agent level with Zero Trust
  • Layer 3 (Prompt): Filters dangerous inputs/outputs at platform level
  • Enables safe AI adoption without productivity-killing bans
"Policy establishes the rules, identity determines who may act,
and prompt-level defense restricts what a user may do."
